# 2023 AHA/ACC Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Patients with Acute Coronary Syndromes

## 1. Initial Evaluation and Diagnostic Triage
Patients presenting with acute chest discomfort or ischemic equivalents should receive immediate 12-lead electrocardiography (ECG) interpreted within 10 minutes of arrival. Serial high-sensitivity cardiac troponin (hs-cTn) measurements at 0 and 1 or 2 hours are recommended to rule in or rule out myocardial injury.

## 2. Antiplatelet and Anticoagulant Pharmacotherapy
All patients with acute coronary syndrome without contraindications should receive chewable non-enteric-coated aspirin (162 to 325 mg) immediately upon presentation. For patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), a potent P2Y12 receptor inhibitor (ticagrelor 180 mg loading dose or prasugrel 60 mg loading dose) is recommended over clopidogrel.
Unfractionated heparin, bivalirudin, or low-molecular-weight heparin should be administered during PCI according to ischemic and bleeding risk stratification.

## 3. High-Intensity Statin Therapy
High-intensity statin therapy (atorvastatin 80 mg daily or rosuvastatin 40 mg daily) should be initiated or continued prior to hospital discharge unless contraindicated, targeting a greater than 50% reduction in LDL cholesterol.

## 4. Contraindications and Critical Precautions
Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), except aspirin, must NOT be initiated and should be discontinued during acute coronary syndrome due to heightened risks of major adverse cardiovascular events, recurrent myocardial infarction, and cardiac rupture.
