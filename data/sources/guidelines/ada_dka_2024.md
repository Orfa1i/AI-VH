# 2024 American Diabetes Association Consensus Statement on Diabetic Ketoacidosis

## 1. Diagnostic Criteria and Severity Assessment
Diabetic ketoacidosis is characterized by the diagnostic triad of hyperglycemia (blood glucose > 200 mg/dL), venous pH < 7.30 or serum bicarbonate < 18 mEq/L, and elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate (> 3.0 mmol/L). Anion gap must be calculated: [Na+] - ([Cl-] + [HCO3-]).

## 2. Intravenous Fluid Resuscitation
Initial fluid management should begin with isotonic crystalloids (balanced crystalloids or 0.9% normal saline) at 500 to 1000 mL/h during the first 2 to 4 hours to restore effective circulating volume. Once blood glucose falls below 200 to 250 mg/dL, 5% dextrose must be added to maintenance fluids to prevent hypoglycemia and allow ongoing insulin infusion for ketosis clearance.

## 3. Potassium Repletion Before Insulin Infusion
Insulin therapy drives extracellular potassium into cells. Intravenous regular insulin MUST be withheld if initial serum potassium is less than 3.3 mEq/L, until adequate potassium repletion (20 to 40 mEq/h) restores levels above 3.3 mEq/L to prevent fatal cardiac arrhythmias.

## 4. Bicarbonate Therapy Cautions
Bicarbonate infusion is not recommended in mild-to-moderate DKA (pH >= 6.9) due to potential risks of paradoxical central nervous system acidosis, hypokalemia, and delayed clearance of blood lactate.
